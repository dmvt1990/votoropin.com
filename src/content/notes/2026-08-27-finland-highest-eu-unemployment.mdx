Finland's unemployment rate was 10.3% in June 2026. Spain's was 10.1%, according to Eurostat's harmonised series released July 30, 2026. Europe's worst labour market is no longer where you would guess.

Since 2010 that position has belonged to Spain or Greece, and briefly before them to Latvia. Finland took it in November 2025 and has held it every month since.

The Finnish rate peaked at 10.8% in May 2026, a level last seen in December 1998. It was 7.2% in January 2023. Spain moved the other way: its quarterly labour force survey, the EPA, put the national unemployment rate at 9.87% in the second quarter of 2026, down 0.96 percentage points in a single quarter, with 22.78 million people in employment (INE, July 28, 2026).

## What sits behind the ranking

Finland's rise has been supply-led. The economy ministry's May 2026 forecast describes labour supply growing considerably while demand stayed subdued, driven particularly by older workers staying in the workforce. Long-term unemployment, meaning a year or more out of work, is forecast to average 141,000 in 2026 and peak near 144,000 over the summer.

Demand is weakening too. Finnish employment fell by 15,000 in the year to June 2026, to 2.658 million (Statistics Finland, July 21, 2026).

The spread is the part worth keeping. In the same June data: Finland 10.3%, Germany 3.9%, Bulgaria and Cyprus 3.0%. Same single market, same measurement standard, a 3.4x range in jobless rates.

*One honest limit: the Finland–Spain gap is 0.2 percentage points, within the range routine revisions can shift. November 2025 was first reported as 10.6% against 10.4% and has since been revised to 10.5% against 10.1%. Spain's second quarter also flatters itself with summer hiring, though employment still grew 0.54% after seasonal adjustment. July 2026 is already in the Eurostat database with Finland back up at 10.5%; Spain's July figure is not yet published.*
