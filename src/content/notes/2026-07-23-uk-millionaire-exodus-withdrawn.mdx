The UK's millionaire exodus was one of 2024's biggest economic stories. The firm behind the number just stopped publishing it.

Henley & Partners said 9,500 millionaires left the UK in 2024 and forecast 16,500 more departures for 2025. The story ran in roughly 10,900 news pieces over 2024, about 30 a day, by the Tax Justice Network's count. Then, in its June 2026 report (dated 2026-06-16), Henley dropped both the forecast and the data provider behind it, New World Wealth, after two forensic reviews flagged the figures as possibly fabricated.

## The official count never matched

The UK's Office for Budget Responsibility projects about 10,800 non-dom departures (residents taxed as domiciled abroad), and HMRC, the tax authority, has tracked at or below that figure. Henley's own 9,500 was just 0.3% of Britain's millionaires.

People really are leaving the UK. But non-doms and millionaires aren't the same population, and the number that would settle the argument doesn't land until HMRC publishes its official count on 30 July 2026.

*One thing that complicates it: "possibly fabricated" is the language used by Tax Policy Associates and the Tax Justice Network in their forensic reviews, not a finding by a court or a regulator, and Henley has not formally conceded it.*
